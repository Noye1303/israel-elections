What's Going On? 🤔

Israel is heading to its first elections since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack — the first time in nearly 40 years an election has been held on its originally scheduled date. This is more than a normal election.

📅

Election Day: October 27, 2026

All 120 seats in the Knesset (parliament) are up for grabs. A party needs at least 61 seats to form a government — usually requiring a coalition of multiple parties.

⚖️

How It Works

You vote for a party, not a person. Parties that win at least 3.25% of the vote get seats (roughly 4 seats). The more votes, the more seats. Then parties try to form a 61-seat coalition.

⚠️ Important: If a party gets less than 3.25% of the vote, they don't enter the Knesset — and all the votes cast for them are completely wasted. They don't count toward any other party. This is why small parties often merge, and why voting for a party hovering near the threshold is a big risk.

🎯

The Big Question

Will Netanyahu stay PM or will Eisenkot (or Bennett) form the next government? Neither bloc can easily reach 61. The Arab parties (12 seats) hold significant kingmaker potential.

🗳️

Historic Moment

First full-term Knesset completion in 50+ years. The Knesset formally dissolved on July 17, 2026. This election will define Israel's path after one of its most traumatic periods.

🔥 The Big Issues

Five burning topics dominate this campaign. Click each to learn more.

⚔️

October 7 Accountability — Who's Responsible?

On October 7, 2023, Hamas launched the deadliest attack on Jews since the Holocaust. The opposition demands an independent state commission of inquiry to investigate government failures. Netanyahu's bloc opposes it, passing their own politically-appointed commission instead. This is a referendum on Netanyahu's leadership.

🌡️ Boiling Hot
🪖

Haredi (Ultra-Orthodox) Military Draft

For decades, ultra-Orthodox Jewish men were exempt from military service to study Torah. There were 400 exemptions in 1948 — today it's hundreds of thousands. The Supreme Court ruled exemptions unconstitutional. With Israel fighting on multiple fronts, secular Israelis are furious. Netanyahu's coalition includes two Haredi parties (Shas, UTJ) who fiercely oppose the draft, creating a massive rift.

🌡️ Boiling Hot
💰

Cost of Living & Housing

Housing prices and everyday costs have skyrocketed. Young families can barely afford to buy homes. War spending has strained the economy. This is the #1 issue for Arab citizens and a top concern for most secular Israelis.

🌡️ Very Hot
🏛️

Judicial Reform / Overhaul

Netanyahu's government tried to dramatically weaken the Supreme Court in 2023, triggering historic protests with over 500,000 marching weekly. The conflict over who controls the judiciary continues to divide Israeli society deeply. Netanyahu is also on trial for corruption — the courts are directly relevant to him personally.

🌡️ Very Hot
🕊️

Hostages & Gaza's Future

All hostages have returned home, but the emotional wound runs deep. Many families feel the government didn't do enough, or sacrificed hostages for political reasons. What happens to Gaza — who governs it, whether settlements expand — is fiercely contested between left and right.

🌡️ Hot

🔵 🔴 The Two Blocs

Israeli politics is roughly divided into two competing blocs, plus the Arab parties who usually don't join either government.

🔴

Netanyahu's Bloc (~52 seats)

Likud + Shas + UTJ + Religious Zionism–Zehut + Otzma Yehudit

Right to far-right. Wants Netanyahu to stay PM. Pro-settlement, opposes a state commission, supports Haredi draft exemptions. Needs 61+ to govern but currently stuck around 51-52.

🔵

Opposition Bloc (~52 seats)

Yashar! + Together + Democrats + Yisrael Beiteinu

Center to center-left. Wants Netanyahu out. Pro-state-commission, pro-Haredi-draft, anti-judicial-overhaul. Also around 52 seats — deadlock! Arab parties (12 seats) could tip the balance.

🟢

Arab Parties (~12 seats)

Joint List + Ra'am

Represent Arab citizens (~20% of population). Usually not invited into governing coalitions. But in 2021, Ra'am joined Bennett/Lapid's coalition — showing it's possible. Their 12 seats could be decisive.

📌 What "Left" and "Right" Mean in Israel

In Israel, left vs. right is less about economics and more about security, land, and religion.

⬅️

Left / Center-Left

More open to a Palestinian state eventually. Support separation from Gaza & West Bank. Pro-democracy, pro-Supreme Court independence. Generally secular. Anti-religious coercion. Support drafting Haredim.

↔️

Center

Security-first, pragmatic. Open to deals with Palestinian Authority but not Hamas. Support drafting Haredim. Mixed on settlements. Pro-democratic institutions. Flexible on religion.

➡️

Right / Far Right

Oppose Palestinian state. Support West Bank settlements and even annexation. Strong military action. Religious nationalist values. Often protect Haredi draft exemptions. Very skeptical of courts.

🏛️ Gaza Tomorrow — What should happen in Gaza after the war? This is one of the most divisive questions in Israeli politics. Positions range from full annexation and Jewish resettlement to Palestinian statehood. Based on official party positions and statements.

📌 Context: After the October 7 attack and the Gaza war, Israel controls around 53% of the Gaza Strip militarily. Hamas still exists but has been significantly weakened. A US-brokered ceasefire began in late 2025. The big question for the next Israeli government: what comes next? Who governs Gaza? Do Israeli troops stay? Are settlements allowed? Is a Palestinian state possible?

🔴 Otzma Yehudit (Ben-Gvir)
Most extreme position
Jewish resettlement of Gaza. Ben-Gvir wants Israel to permanently occupy and resettle Gaza with Jewish communities — essentially reversing the 2005 disengagement. He supports encouraging Palestinian emigration from Gaza. He left Netanyahu's coalition specifically because he opposed the hostage deal and ceasefire, demanding total military victory with no compromise. Opposes any Palestinian governance in Gaza whatsoever.
🔴 Religious Zionism–Zehut (Smotrich)
Annexation position
Full Israeli annexation of Gaza and West Bank. Smotrich supports Israeli sovereignty over Gaza, opposes any Palestinian state, and has pushed to strangle the Palestinian Authority financially. As Finance Minister he oversaw rapid settlement expansion in the West Bank. His vision is a "Greater Israel" where Gaza is absorbed as Israeli territory, with Palestinian residents living under Israeli military administration permanently.
🟤 Likud (Netanyahu)
Deliberate ambiguity
No Palestinian state — but no clear "day after" plan. Netanyahu has explicitly ruled out a Palestinian state and opposes the Palestinian Authority governing Gaza. He wants continued Israeli military presence and security control. He has deliberately avoided a clear post-war plan, which analysts say is partly to keep his far-right coalition partners satisfied. Israel currently controls 53% of Gaza militarily and Netanyahu shows no signs of withdrawing.
🔵 Shas & UTJ (Haredi parties)
Follow Likud's lead
Against Palestinian state, support military control. The Haredi parties largely defer to Netanyahu and the security establishment on Gaza policy. Their primary concern is ensuring Haredi draft exemptions are maintained — they are less ideologically driven on Gaza specifics than Smotrich or Ben-Gvir, but would support any coalition position that keeps their community protected.
🩵 Yashar! (Eisenkot)
Security-first pragmatist
No Hamas, no annexation — pragmatic security arrangement. As a former IDF Chief of Staff, Eisenkot is focused on achievable security outcomes rather than ideology. He supports demilitarising Gaza and removing Hamas from power, but does not support Jewish resettlement. He has not committed to a Palestinian state but has spoken about the need for a regional arrangement involving Arab states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Jordan) in Gaza's governance. Opposes annexation.
🔵 Together (Bennett + Lapid)
Separation without statehood (now)
Separate from Gaza, no Palestinian state in current climate. Bennett historically led the 2005 opposition to Gaza withdrawal as a settler movement figure, but now supports separation. Together's platform focuses on "the day after" involving Arab state involvement in rebuilding and governing Gaza — similar to the Abraham Accords approach. They do not support a Palestinian state under current conditions but want a regional solution, not annexation.
🔵 Yisrael Beiteinu (Lieberman)
Land swap concept
Territorial separation and population exchange. Lieberman has long supported the idea of land swaps — Arab-majority areas of Israel proper going to a Palestinian entity in exchange for settlement blocs in the West Bank being annexed to Israel. On Gaza specifically, he opposes both Hamas governance and Jewish resettlement, and supports a buffer zone with Arab state involvement in reconstruction. Hawkish but pragmatic.
🟣 The Democrats (Golan)
Most progressive Zionist position
Two-state solution — the only Zionist party to say it openly. Yair Golan is the only Jewish-majority party leader to explicitly support a two-state outcome after October 7 — though he says the approach must be "nuanced" given current realities. The Democrats support halting annexation in the West Bank, ending the war in Gaza, and advancing territorial separation from the Palestinians as the only long-term solution. They support a reformed Palestinian Authority eventually governing Gaza.
🟢 Joint List (Hadash–Ta'al)
Full Palestinian rights
Full withdrawal and Palestinian state. The Joint List supports a full Israeli withdrawal from Gaza and the West Bank, a Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as its capital, and the Palestinian right of return. They oppose all Israeli military presence in Gaza and call for an immediate ceasefire and reconstruction under Palestinian governance. They are consistent advocates for Palestinian rights but have zero influence in coalition negotiations.
🟢 Ra'am (Mansour Abbas)
Pragmatic Arab voice
Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as capital. Ra'am officially supports a Palestinian state and opposes Israeli occupation. However, Abbas is the most pragmatic Arab politician — he joined a government in 2021 and focuses on delivering concrete benefits for Arab citizens inside Israel. On Gaza, he supports a Palestinian Authority-governed solution and Palestinian statehood, but is more willing than other Arab parties to work within Israel's political system to advance it.

📖 Israeli Politics — Plain English Guide

New to Israeli politics? Here are the key terms you need to know.

🏛️ The Knesset

Israel's parliament, with 120 seats. You vote for a party, not a person. If a party wins 10% of votes, they get 12 seats. Then parties negotiate to form a coalition government of 61+ seats.

📊 Electoral Threshold

A party must win at least 3.25% of the total vote to enter the Knesset — roughly 4 seats. If they fall short, all their votes are wasted and redistributed. This is why small parties merge!

🤝 Coalition Government

No party wins a majority alone. After elections, the President tasks the most likely leader to form a coalition. They negotiate with other parties, offering cabinet seats and policy concessions in exchange for support.

🕍 Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox)

Ultra-Orthodox Jews who follow a strictly traditional religious lifestyle. Men typically study Torah full-time, don't serve in the army, and have large families. They make up ~13% of Israel's population and are a decisive voting bloc.

✡️ Religious Zionism

Jews who combine Orthodox religious practice with strong Zionist national ideology — believing settling the whole Land of Israel is a religious commandment. Different from Haredim, who are often non-Zionist or anti-Zionist.

⚖️ Judicial Overhaul

Netanyahu's 2023 plan to weaken Israel's Supreme Court — reducing its ability to strike down laws and appointing judges through political committees. Caused Israel's biggest-ever protests (500k+ weekly). The conflict continues in 2026.

🪖 Reserve Duty (Miluim)

Israeli citizens (mostly men) must do annual reserve military service. Since Oct 7, many have served months instead of weeks. Secular Israelis resent that Haredi men are exempt — creating deep social tension.

🕊️ Two-State Solution

The idea of creating an independent Palestinian state alongside Israel. Mainstream Israeli politics has shifted significantly — even most centrists now say "not now" or "not with Hamas." Left-wing parties are the only ones explicitly supporting it long-term.

🏘️ Settlements

Israeli Jewish communities built in the West Bank (territories occupied since 1967). The international community generally considers them illegal. Right-wing parties support their expansion; left-wing parties oppose it. ~700,000 Israelis live in settlements.

🔴 Netanyahu Bloc

The group of right-wing parties that support Netanyahu staying as PM: Likud, Shas, UTJ, Religious Zionism, and Otzma Yehudit. They need 61+ seats to govern but are stuck at ~52.

🔵 Opposition Bloc

Parties that want Netanyahu out: Yashar!, Together, Democrats, Yisrael Beiteinu. Also stuck at ~52. They need Arab party support (which is complicated) or defections from the right to form a government.

📋 State Commission of Inquiry

An independent judicial investigation into what went wrong before/during October 7. Opposition demands it; Netanyahu's government opposes it (proposing a politically appointed commission instead). Whether a PM who was in office during a failure can control the inquiry into that failure is deeply controversial.

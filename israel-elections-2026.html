What's Going On? 🤔

Israel is heading to its first elections since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack — the first time in nearly 40 years an election has been held on its originally scheduled date. This is more than a normal election.

📅

Election Day: October 27, 2026

All 120 seats in the Knesset (parliament) are up for grabs. A party needs at least 61 seats to form a government — usually requiring a coalition of multiple parties.

⚖️

How It Works

You vote for a party, not a person. Parties that win at least 3.25% of the vote get seats (roughly 4 seats). The more votes, the more seats. Then parties try to form a 61-seat coalition.

🎯

The Big Question

Will Netanyahu stay PM or will Eisenkot (or Bennett) form the next government? Neither bloc can easily reach 61. The Arab parties (12 seats) hold significant kingmaker potential.

🗳️

Historic Moment

First full-term Knesset completion in 50+ years. The Knesset formally dissolved on July 17, 2026. This election will define Israel's path after one of its most traumatic periods.

🔥 The Big Issues

Five burning topics dominate this campaign. Click each to learn more.

⚔️

October 7 Accountability — Who's Responsible?

On October 7, 2023, Hamas launched the deadliest attack on Jews since the Holocaust. The opposition demands an independent state commission of inquiry to investigate government failures. Netanyahu's bloc opposes it, passing their own politically-appointed commission instead. This is a referendum on Netanyahu's leadership.

🌡️ Boiling Hot
🪖

Haredi (Ultra-Orthodox) Military Draft

For decades, ultra-Orthodox Jewish men were exempt from military service to study Torah. There were 400 exemptions in 1948 — today it's hundreds of thousands. The Supreme Court ruled exemptions unconstitutional. With Israel fighting on multiple fronts, secular Israelis are furious. Netanyahu's coalition includes two Haredi parties (Shas, UTJ) who fiercely oppose the draft, creating a massive rift.

🌡️ Boiling Hot
💰

Cost of Living & Housing

Housing prices and everyday costs have skyrocketed. Young families can barely afford to buy homes. War spending has strained the economy. This is the #1 issue for Arab citizens and a top concern for most secular Israelis.

🌡️ Very Hot
🏛️

Judicial Reform / Overhaul

Netanyahu's government tried to dramatically weaken the Supreme Court in 2023, triggering historic protests with over 500,000 marching weekly. The conflict over who controls the judiciary continues to divide Israeli society deeply. Netanyahu is also on trial for corruption — the courts are directly relevant to him personally.

🌡️ Very Hot
🕊️

Hostages & Gaza's Future

All hostages have returned home, but the emotional wound runs deep. Many families feel the government didn't do enough, or sacrificed hostages for political reasons. What happens to Gaza — who governs it, whether settlements expand — is fiercely contested between left and right.

🌡️ Hot

🔵 🔴 The Two Blocs

Israeli politics is roughly divided into two competing blocs, plus the Arab parties who usually don't join either government.

🔴

Netanyahu's Bloc (~52 seats)

Likud + Shas + UTJ + Religious Zionism–Zehut + Otzma Yehudit

Right to far-right. Wants Netanyahu to stay PM. Pro-settlement, opposes a state commission, supports Haredi draft exemptions. Needs 61+ to govern but currently stuck around 51-52.

🔵

Opposition Bloc (~52 seats)

Yashar! + Together + Democrats + Yisrael Beiteinu

Center to center-left. Wants Netanyahu out. Pro-state-commission, pro-Haredi-draft, anti-judicial-overhaul. Also around 52 seats — deadlock! Arab parties (12 seats) could tip the balance.

🟢

Arab Parties (~12 seats)

Joint List + Ra'am

Represent Arab citizens (~20% of population). Usually not invited into governing coalitions. But in 2021, Ra'am joined Bennett/Lapid's coalition — showing it's possible. Their 12 seats could be decisive.

📌 What "Left" and "Right" Mean in Israel

In Israel, left vs. right is less about economics and more about security, land, and religion.

⬅️

Left / Center-Left

More open to a Palestinian state eventually. Support separation from Gaza & West Bank. Pro-democracy, pro-Supreme Court independence. Generally secular. Anti-religious coercion. Support drafting Haredim.

↔️

Center

Security-first, pragmatic. Open to deals with Palestinian Authority but not Hamas. Support drafting Haredim. Mixed on settlements. Pro-democratic institutions. Flexible on religion.

➡️

Right / Far Right

Oppose Palestinian state. Support West Bank settlements and even annexation. Strong military action. Religious nationalist values. Often protect Haredi draft exemptions. Very skeptical of courts.

ישר! — "Straight!"
👤 Gadi Eisenkot (Former IDF Chief of Staff)
~23 seats
The new frontrunner — a former top general promising accountability, unity, and a new chapter after October 7. Currently the largest party in polls.
What They Believe
✅ State Commission of Inquiry (Oct 7) 🪖 Haredi Draft (moderate approach) 🏛️ Protect Supreme Court 🤝 Open to Shas in coalition 🔒 Strong security 💰 Lower cost of living
The Story

Eisenkot was IDF Chief of Staff, then joined Benny Gantz's Blue & White. After resigning in 2025, he built Yashar from scratch. Uniquely, he takes a softer line on Haredi draft than other opposition parties and has signaled he'd sit with Shas — making him more coalition-flexible. His military credibility gives him broad appeal across security-minded voters. He's led polls for months, sometimes overtaking Likud itself.

What It Means for Different Groups
💻 Tech Workers ✅ Good
Supports democratic institutions and rule of law — critical for Israel's tech sector reputation. Pro-economy reforms. More stable governance appeals to investors and multinationals operating in Israel.
👨‍👩‍👧 Young Families ✅ Good
Housing and cost-of-living are platform priorities. Security credibility means parents feel safer. Supports Haredi draft — meaning more people share the military burden, reducing burden on secular sons.
🪖 Soldiers & Reservists ✅ Very Good
Led by a former IDF chief. Strongly supports accountability for Oct 7 failures. Supports Haredi draft — reducing the unfair burden on secular soldiers. Reservists are burned out; Yashar prioritizes this.
🕍 Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox) 🔶 Mixed
Unlike other opposition parties, Eisenkot has a softer stance on the Haredi draft — willing to negotiate rather than force. He's open to sitting with Shas (Sephardi Haredi party). Still, Haredim largely won't vote for him.
🕌 Arab Citizens 🔶 Mixed
No specific Arab-focused policies, but supports rule of law and anti-discrimination broadly. His military background and security-first approach doesn't specifically address Arab community priorities.
🏭 Non-Tech Workers 🔶 Mixed
Cost-of-living agenda helps all workers. Less clear on specific labor protections. More stability could help wages. Main appeal is national security and institutional trust.

Together (B'Yachad) Center
ביחד — Bennett + Lapid alliance
👤 Naftali Bennett (Former PM) + Yair Lapid (Former PM)
~13 seats
Two former prime ministers united. Bennett leads, Lapid is #2. Centrist alliance focused on military equality, cost of living, and keeping Israel democratic.
What They Believe
✅ Equality of military service 🏛️ Protect democracy 💰 Lower cost of living 🔍 State Commission of Inquiry ⚖️ Judicial independence 🇮🇱 Jewish & democratic state
The Story

Bennett (right-wing settler background turned centrist-pragmatist) and Lapid (secular centrist) previously formed Israel's unity government (2021-22). They merged their parties in April 2026. Bennett, despite his religious Zionist roots, strongly supports drafting Haredim and built a reputation for pragmatic, technocratic governance. The combination gives them broad appeal from moderate right to center-left.

What It Means for Different Groups
💻Tech Workers✅ Good
Bennett was beloved by hi-tech — a former tech entrepreneur himself. His government promoted tech, low regulation, and innovation. Lapid is also pro-business. Strong appeal to Israel's startup nation.
👨‍👩‍👧Young Families✅ Good
Housing affordability is central. Military equality means sons of secular families won't carry the draft burden alone. Cost-of-living is a top platform priority.
🪖Soldiers & Reservists✅ Very Good
Strongest proponent of Haredi draft equality. Reservists carrying a huge burden feel very strongly about this. Bennett and Lapid both support drafting all eligible citizens.
🕍Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox)❌ Bad
Hard no. Together strongly supports forcing Haredim to serve in the military or do national service. Haredim see this as an existential threat to their way of life and Torah study culture.
🕌Arab Citizens🔶 Mixed
Lapid's government (2021-22) included Ra'am — the first Arab party in an Israeli coalition. Some progress on Arab community investment. But military equality focus doesn't include Arab citizens (who are generally exempt from service).
🏭Non-Tech Workers🔶 Mixed
Cost-of-living agenda helps, but the party's hi-tech-friendly reputation can feel distant from manual and service workers. Broader economic reforms not as clearly detailed.
LK
Likud Center-Right
הליכוד — "The Consolidation"
👤 Benjamin Netanyahu (Current PM) — in office since 1996 (off & on)
~22 seats
Israel's dominant right-wing party for 50 years, now fighting to stay in power amid slipping polls. Founded 1973. Netanyahu has led it since 1993 (mostly).
What They Believe
🔴 Strong military, no concessions to Hamas 🏘️ Pro-West Bank settlements 💪 Free market, private sector ❌ Opposes independent commission (Oct 7) 🤝 Close alliance with Trump/USA ⚖️ Weaken court oversight
The Story

Netanyahu is on trial for corruption (bribery, fraud, breach of trust) — a fact that dominates politics. He's the longest-serving PM in Israeli history. His Likud ran the judicial overhaul, which caused massive protests in 2023. After Oct 7, his "security reputation" took a huge hit. He's tried to brand this election around Trump, Iran, and the idea of a "broad national government." The party is slipping — down from 32 seats in 2022 to ~22 now.

What It Means for Different Groups
💻Tech Workers❌ Concerning
The judicial overhaul triggered a "brain drain" — hundreds of hi-tech companies moved funds abroad. Many tech CEOs publicly opposed it. Continued weakening of courts is bad for investor confidence in a rule-of-law democracy.
👨‍👩‍👧Young Families🔶 Mixed
Some economic aid programs, pro-natalist benefits. But high cost of living, housing crisis, and economic uncertainty from years of instability are largely attributed to their governance.
🪖Soldiers & Reservists❌ Bad
Haredim in the coalition mean no mandatory draft for ultra-Orthodox. Reservists are serving repeated long stints because of the manpower gap. Many soldiers feel Likud protected political allies over military fairness.
🕍Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox)✅ Good
Likud governs with Shas and UTJ, protecting Haredi interests. No forced draft, continued yeshiva funding. Many Haredim vote for Shas/UTJ directly, but Likud is their governmental protector.
🕌Arab Citizens❌ Bad
Coalition partners Ben-Gvir and Smotrich have made inflammatory statements about Arabs. Likud's nationalist direction is seen as threatening to Arab civil equality. Police behavior in Arab communities is a major concern.
✡️Settlers & National Religious✅ Good
Supports West Bank settlements, opposes withdrawal. Under this government, 13 new settlements were approved. National-religious voters see Likud as their protector in government.

The Democrats Left
הדמוקרטים — Labor + Meretz merger
👤 Yair Golan (Former Deputy IDF Chief)
~10 seats
A merger of Israel's traditional left (Labor) and the progressive left (Meretz). Social-democratic values, strong civil rights, most progressive on Palestinian issue.
What They Believe
🕊️ Two-state solution (long term) ⚖️ Strong Supreme Court 🏛️ Social democracy 🌈 LGBTQ+ rights 🏘️ Arab-Jewish coexistence 🔍 Full accountability for Oct 7
The Story

Israel's left has collapsed over decades — from dominant in the 1970s to a minor force. Merging Labor and Meretz was a survival move. Yair Golan, a general turned politician, leads them — giving military credibility to a left-wing platform. They strongly oppose Netanyahu and are the strongest voice for Palestinian rights and democratic values among Zionist parties.

What It Means for Different Groups
💻Tech Workers✅ Good
Strong rule-of-law stance, pro-democracy, and rights-based governance. Many secular, educated tech workers align ideologically. Social-progressive values resonate in hi-tech culture.
🕌Arab Citizens✅ Good
Most sympathetic to Arab civil rights among Zionist parties. Supports equality legislation. Has worked with Arab parties before. Still Zionist — won't support binational state.
🕍Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox)❌ Very Bad
The Democrats support mandatory drafting of Haredim, elimination of religious exemptions from marriage law, and LGBTQ+ rights — all anathema to Haredi values.
✡️Settlers & National Religious❌ Very Bad
Support eventual Palestinian state, oppose settlement expansion. Settlers see them as existential opponents. Strong divide here.
YB
Yisrael Beiteinu Secular Right
ישראל ביתנו — "Israel Is Our Home"
👤 Avigdor Lieberman (Former Defense/Finance Minister)
~10 seats
The secular nationalist party, originally built on Russian-speaking immigrants. Anti-religious-coercion, pro-Haredi-draft, and hawkish on security. The Haredi parties' arch-nemesis.
What They Believe
🪖 Mandatory Haredi draft ⛪ Separate religion from state 🔒 Very hawkish security 🏛️ Civil marriage 🔍 State Commission (Oct 7) 🇮🇱 Strong national identity
The Story

Lieberman was Netanyahu's ally for decades, then became his fiercest opponent — largely over Haredi draft laws. He's blunt, aggressive in style, and has a loyal secular-Russian base. Represents Israelis who are both nationalist (hawkish, anti-Palestinian concessions) AND anti-religion-in-politics — a combination that's unique in Israeli politics.

What It Means for Different Groups
🪖Soldiers & Reservists✅ Very Good
Lieberman has been the loudest, most consistent voice for Haredi draft equality. Reservists see him as their champion. He left the coalition over this issue.
🕍Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox)❌ Hates Them
Lieberman calls Haredi leadership a "political extortion machine." He wants to cut yeshiva funding, draft Haredi men, and end religious monopoly over marriage. They see him as an existential enemy.
🇷🇺Russian-Speaking Immigrants✅ Good
His historic base. Civil marriage (not just religious) matters enormously to Russian immigrants, many of whom aren't considered Jewish by rabbinical law. Lieberman fights for their rights consistently.
🕌Arab Citizens❌ Bad
Nationalist, hawkish. Has made provocative statements about Arab communities. Not focused on Arab civil rights. His security positions are very hardline.
SH
Shas Sephardi Haredi
ש״ס — Sephardi Torah Guardians
👤 Aryeh Deri (Convicted of tax offenses, controversial reinstatement)
~7 seats
The political home of Sephardi (Middle Eastern-origin) ultra-Orthodox Jews. Deeply religious, socially conservative, and fiercely protective of Haredi draft exemptions. In Netanyahu's coalition.
What They Believe
📚 Torah study as national value ❌ No Haredi draft 🕍 Religious marriage & conversion law 💙 Social welfare programs ⚖️ Weaken courts (oppose judicial review) 🔒 Anti-Palestinian state
The Story

Founded by the late Rabbi Ovadia Yosef in 1984, Shas represents Sephardi/Mizrahi Haredim who faced discrimination from the Ashkenazi establishment. They're not just religious — they also advocate for social welfare for the poor. Deri's story is dramatic: convicted of bribery in 2000, returned to politics, then convicted again of tax offenses, yet returned as Interior Minister — only to be dismissed by Supreme Court order.

What It Means for Different Groups
🕍Haredim (Sephardi)✅ Their Party
This IS the Sephardi Haredi party. Protects yeshiva funding, draft exemptions, religious law in marriage and conversion. Deeply aligned with their community values.
🪖Soldiers & Reservists❌ Very Bad
The core opponent of Haredi draft equality. Actively passed laws to protect draft evaders from arrest. Reservists doing months of reserve duty while Haredim study Torah — Shas defends this arrangement.
💰Low-Income Families🔶 Mixed
Shas historically advocates for social welfare and supports poor families (many Sephardi communities). But Haredi families tend to have many children and low participation in workforce — welfare dependency is complex.
🌈LGBTQ+ Community❌ Very Bad
Deeply opposed. Shas represents the traditional religious view that homosexuality is forbidden. They oppose same-sex adoption, marriage equality, and have made hostile statements about LGBTQ+ Israelis.
UT
United Torah Judaism (UTJ) Ashkenazi Haredi
יהדות התורה המאוחדת
👤 Yitzhak Goldknopf & Moshe Gafni (joint leadership)
~7-8 seats
The Ashkenazi (Eastern European-origin) Haredi party. Even more strictly religious than Shas. Coalition partner with Netanyahu. The most insular, internally-focused ultra-Orthodox party.
What They Believe
📖 Torah study above all ❌ Absolute opposition to Haredi draft 🏫 Autonomous Haredi education system ⚖️ Religious courts over civil courts 📵 Anti-internet / anti-smartphone 🧓 Led by rabbinical council, not voters
The Story

UTJ is actually an alliance of two sub-parties: Agudat Yisrael (Hassidic streams) and Degel Hatorah (Lithuanian/Litvish). Their political direction is set by senior rabbis, not elected politicians. They fight for maximum Haredi autonomy — separate schools (no secular curriculum), no military service, no internet. They passed the controversial "Basic Law: Torah Study" to constitutionally entrench yeshiva funding.

What It Means for Different Groups
🕍Haredim (Ashkenazi)✅ Their Party
UTJ is THE Ashkenazi Haredi party. Maximum protection for Torah study, exemptions, funding, and lifestyle. Rabbis direct the party. Haredim trust it completely.
💻Tech Workers❌ Bad
UTJ actively opposes internet access in Haredi schools, tech literacy, and secular education. Their governance priorities are completely misaligned with the tech sector's values and Israel's innovation economy.
👩Women's Rights❌ Bad
Traditional gender roles. Women serve mainly as homemakers and mothers in Haredi ideology. Opposition to women serving in military roles or advancing in religious courts (in agunah/divorce law cases, women are severely disadvantaged).
RZ
Religious Zionism–Zehut Far Right
ציונות דתית–זהות
👤 Bezalel Smotrich (Finance Minister)
~5-6 seats
Hard-line nationalist religious Zionism. Pro-settlement, anti-Palestinian, and theocratic tendencies. Running with libertarian Zehut to cross the electoral threshold.
What They Believe
🏘️ Settle all of "Land of Israel" ❌ No Palestinian state ever 🙏 Jewish law should govern Israel ⚖️ Gut the Supreme Court 🔥 Total victory in Gaza 🇺🇸 Pro-Trump, anti-Biden-era USA
The Story

Smotrich is Finance Minister and wants to annex the West Bank. He's the ideological architect of the judicial overhaul. Ran with libertarian Zehut (Moshe Feiglin) to boost their combined vote above the 3.25% threshold — an unusual pairing of religious nationalism with libertarianism. They're slipping in polls but still likely to pass the threshold.

What It Means for Different Groups
✡️Settlers & National Religious✅ Their Champion
Smotrich's party exists to serve settler interests. Expansion of settlements, annexation ideology, and a religious-nationalist vision of Israel as a Jewish state in a Biblical sense.
🕌Arab Citizens❌ Extremely Bad
Smotrich has made statements about erasing a Palestinian village and denied Palestinian nationhood on stage. Arab citizens face threats to equal rights under Religious Zionism's vision of a maximally Jewish state.
💻Tech Workers❌ Bad
International isolation risk — their policies on settlements and courts alarm Western democracies and could trigger economic sanctions or diplomatic isolation that hurts tech exports and R&D partnerships.
OY
Otzma Yehudit Far Right
עוצמה יהודית — "Jewish Power"
👤 Itamar Ben-Gvir (Former National Security Minister)
~7-9 seats
Israel's most extreme mainstream party. Ben-Gvir is controversial, provocative, and previously convicted of incitement to racism. His rise is a sign of deep frustration in parts of Israeli society.
What They Believe
🔥 Maximum force in Gaza 🏘️ Encourage Arab emigration ⚔️ Annexation of Gaza & West Bank 🚫 Ban "disloyal" Arab citizens 🙏 Jewish supremacy in public spaces 💪 Vigilante Jewish defense groups
The Story

Ben-Gvir hung a portrait of Baruch Goldstein (who massacred 29 Palestinians in 1994) in his home until forced to remove it. He was barred from IDF service for "extremism." Yet he served as National Security Minister under Netanyahu, significantly expanding police powers. He left the coalition when Netanyahu agreed to a Gaza hostage deal. His base is young, Sephardi-Haredi-adjacent, and deeply frustrated with Israel's direction.

What It Means for Different Groups
🕌Arab Citizens❌ Existential Threat
Ben-Gvir has called for expulsion of "disloyal" Arab citizens, expanded police stops in Arab areas, and promoted Jewish supremacy in public spaces. Arab Israelis view his rise as a direct threat to their safety and civil rights.
✡️Hardline Nationalists✅ Their Man
Supporters feel Israel has been too soft for too long. After Oct 7, his message of maximum force and Jewish strength resonates with a portion of Israelis who want uncompromising action.
🌍International Standing❌ Very Bad
His participation in government caused the US and EU to issue warnings. Israel's international reputation suffers significantly. This affects trade, diplomacy, and the tech sector's global partnerships.

The loudest Knesset voice for Arab civil rights, anti-discrimination legislation, investment in Arab communities, and Palestinian rights. Many Arab citizens feel unrepresented otherwise.
🏭Non-Tech Workers✅ Good (in theory)
Strong labor rights platform. Hadash has communist roots and advocates for workers' rights, unions, and wealth redistribution. But limited actual power in coalition.

📖 Israeli Politics — Plain English Guide

New to Israeli politics? Here are the key terms you need to know.

🏛️ The Knesset

Israel's parliament, with 120 seats. You vote for a party, not a person. If a party wins 10% of votes, they get 12 seats. Then parties negotiate to form a coalition government of 61+ seats.

📊 Electoral Threshold

A party must win at least 3.25% of the total vote to enter the Knesset — roughly 4 seats. If they fall short, all their votes are wasted and redistributed. This is why small parties merge!

🤝 Coalition Government

No party wins a majority alone. After elections, the President tasks the most likely leader to form a coalition. They negotiate with other parties, offering cabinet seats and policy concessions in exchange for support.

🕍 Haredim (Ultra-Orthodox)

Ultra-Orthodox Jews who follow a strictly traditional religious lifestyle. Men typically study Torah full-time, don't serve in the army, and have large families. They make up ~13% of Israel's population and are a decisive voting bloc.

✡️ Religious Zionism

Jews who combine Orthodox religious practice with strong Zionist national ideology — believing settling the whole Land of Israel is a religious commandment. Different from Haredim, who are often non-Zionist or anti-Zionist.

⚖️ Judicial Overhaul

Netanyahu's 2023 plan to weaken Israel's Supreme Court — reducing its ability to strike down laws and appointing judges through political committees. Caused Israel's biggest-ever protests (500k+ weekly). The conflict continues in 2026.

🪖 Reserve Duty (Miluim)

Israeli citizens (mostly men) must do annual reserve military service. Since Oct 7, many have served months instead of weeks. Secular Israelis resent that Haredi men are exempt — creating deep social tension.

🕊️ Two-State Solution

The idea of creating an independent Palestinian state alongside Israel. Mainstream Israeli politics has shifted significantly — even most centrists now say "not now" or "not with Hamas." Left-wing parties are the only ones explicitly supporting it long-term.

🏘️ Settlements

Israeli Jewish communities built in the West Bank (territories occupied since 1967). The international community generally considers them illegal. Right-wing parties support their expansion; left-wing parties oppose it. ~700,000 Israelis live in settlements.

🔴 Netanyahu Bloc

The group of right-wing parties that support Netanyahu staying as PM: Likud, Shas, UTJ, Religious Zionism, and Otzma Yehudit. They need 61+ seats to govern but are stuck at ~52.

🔵 Opposition Bloc

Parties that want Netanyahu out: Yashar!, Together, Democrats, Yisrael Beiteinu. Also stuck at ~52. They need Arab party support (which is complicated) or defections from the right to form a government.

📋 State Commission of Inquiry

An independent judicial investigation into what went wrong before/during October 7. Opposition demands it; Netanyahu's government opposes it (proposing a politically appointed commission instead). Whether a PM who was in office during a failure can control the inquiry into that failure is deeply controversial.
